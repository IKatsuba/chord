# CHD-172 — chord draws its own selection and copies it

Since CHD-31, chord reads the mouse so that the wheel scrolls the
transcript. A plain drag therefore selects nothing in the terminal, and
chord drew nothing either: every `<text>` and the textarea were
`selectable: false` (`src/ui/theme/text.ts`). All a person got was the
status line's `shift + drag to select text` (`src/ui/app/drag.ts`). The
owner found that strange (CHD-172, comment #8105). Claude Code 2.1.292 is
the reference. A drag paints the cells it covers on a slate background
(`48;5;66`). Release copies them, and the status row says
`copied 59 chars to clipboard · disable auto-copy in /config`.
`copyOnSelect` (on by default) turns auto-copy off, and then a key copies.

This design builds that on OpenTUI 0.6.0's own selection and clipboard
service, both used as they are. The owner chose every open question on
2026-10-15. The drafts, the critic's review and the spike captures are in
`evidence/CHD-172/drafts/`; the older files beside `drafts/` belong to a
quarry task that once had this number. Every spike stubbed the clipboard
except protocol's first, which wrote the owner's real one. For that reason
the tests get a guard (below).

## The decisions, in short

| Question | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| Whose selection | **OpenTUI's**, as it is: the renderer's `Selection`, painted by each selectable text and the textarea. No selection component of chord's. |
| Mouse modes | **1000 + 1002 + 1006.** OpenTUI already writes 1002 (and 1003). chord sets `enableMouseMovement: false`, a documented option, so hover motion (1003) goes. |
| What is selectable | What the agent or the person wrote: replies, prompts, messages, tool output, diffs' code, the header's lines, the draft. Not chord's furniture: gutters, the mark, line numbers, the fold marker, borders, the status line, lists, boxes. |
| The highlight | A `selection` palette role on every selectable text: `ansi256(66)` on dark themes, `ansi256(152)` on light ones, and a slate under `NO_COLOR` and the 16-colour theme too (owner, 8a). |
| Release | Copies when `copyOnSelect` is on. The highlight stays until the note goes, 3 s later (owner, 6b). |
| The clipboard | OpenTUI's `createClipboard`, `best-available`: the system clipboard natively, with OSC 52 when that fails, and OSC 52 alone over SSH (owner, 1a). No pbcopy. |
| The note | `copied N chars to clipboard`. When only OSC 52 went: `sent N chars to the terminal's clipboard` (owner, 2a). N counts graphemes. |
| The setting | `copyOnSelect`, boolean, default `true`, user's file only, read at start. The settings file is the only toggle (owner, 3a). |
| The copy key | **ctrl+c while a selection is shown**, plus a rebindable `selection:copy` on ctrl+shift+c (owner, 4a). The panel says `ctrl + c to copy`. |
| The draft | Selectable. A key or a paste clears the selection before the textarea hears it, so nothing is replaced (owner, 5a). |
| A drag and the view | Following stops while the button is down. A wheel step during a drag extends the selection. There is no autoscroll at the edges (owner, 9a). |
| What a copy copies | What is highlighted at the moment of the copy, read through `getSelectedText()`. A reply keeps chord's own wrapping (owner, 7a; CHD-396 follows up). |
| `selectHint` | Goes, with its timer. The bypass modifier stays in the README and the guide. |
| Agent, record, MCP | Untouched. Nothing of a selection or a copy reaches the agent, the session record or chord's tools. |

## What OpenTUI does, and what was tried

Everything was run in scratch copies of the tree under `mktemp -d`, on the
fake agent, through tmux with raw SGR reports and through
`tests/ui/harness.tsx`. The full account is in the drafts and the
`spike-*.txt` captures.

- **The highlight.** A drag paints the covered cells live, in the
  `selectionBg` each text is given. `48;5;66` came out exactly as Claude
  Code's. Without a `selectionBg`, OpenTUI swaps fg and bg, and text in
  the terminal's default foreground turns black. The terminal's default
  colours swapped draw nothing. That is why every palette, `NO_COLOR`'s
  included, gets a real colour. Double click selects a word and triple
  click a line, and both come along at no cost.
- **How a selection is held.** The anchor is stored relative to the
  renderable the drag started in. The focus is the pointer's screen cell.
  Each text keeps its own local range. A layout move (scrolling, a box
  growing below) carries the highlight with its text. A change of text
  inside a renderable keeps the cells and changes what they hold
  (`spike-runtime-mutations.txt`). The first round's reading, that the
  highlight and the copy fall out of step on a scroll, was wrong. The
  captures show content moving off screen, or the range moving on the next
  motion report (critic, finding 2).
- **The text it gives.** OpenTUI's own soft wrap (a prompt) is joined back
  into one line. chord's markdown lays replies out itself, so a wrapped
  reply copies as the screen's rows, with a newline and the hanging
  indent; table and code-block borders are text and come along. A blank
  row between blocks is dropped. Two renderables side by side are glued
  with no gap (`⏺Edit(…)`, `1+ /**`, the mark's half blocks against the
  version line), which is why gutters and line numbers are not
  selectable. A fold copies as drawn: the `… +N lines` marker, not the
  hidden lines. The status line copies empty, because its content is
  replaced under the selection.
- **The prompt box.** The textarea's selection is an editor's: a key, a
  backspace or a paste replaces the selected text, and shift+←/→ select.
  A drag from the transcript into the draft selects both, as one
  selection. The border and the `›` gutter start nothing.
- **Mouse modes.** chord's renderer writes
  `?1000h ?1002h ?1003h ?1006h` today (`spike-runtime-modes.txt`).
  CHD-31's "1002 in place of 1000" came with OpenTUI.
  `enableMouseMovement: false` gives `?1000h ?1002h ?1006h`.
  `"mouse": false` writes none.
- **Cost.** `selectable` on every text costs nothing per frame (0.90 ms
  against 1.09 ms at 400 texts). A hover report under 1003 costs about
  15 µs and no frame. A selecting drag costs about 100 µs and one frame a
  report.
- **The clipboard.** `createHostClipboard` writes the system clipboard
  through `libopentui`: NSPasteboard on macOS, X11 and Wayland on Linux,
  no `pbcopy`, `xclip` or `wl-copy`. It worked from source, compiled and
  inside tmux. `createRendererClipboardAdapter` writes OSC 52 and can only
  report `attempted`. A terminal that drops OSC 52 says nothing, so a
  refusal is never seen, and the task's "pbcopy where OSC 52 is refused"
  cannot be built (owner, 1a). Inside tmux, OpenTUI wraps OSC 52 in DCS
  passthrough, which a default tmux drops; `allow-passthrough on` lets it
  through.

## The lifecycle

chord keeps, in the window's half of `app/state.ts`, one `selection`
value: `none`, `dragging`, `shown` or `copying`. While dragging, it also
keeps `wasFollowing` and `moved`. `src/ui/app/selection.ts` (pure, in
place of `drag.ts`) maps each observed fact to actions. One hook in
`src/ui/app/terminal.ts` observes the facts, through public API only.
OpenTUI emits `selection` only when a drag finishes. Its own clears fire
no event, so the hook reads `renderer.hasSelection` on the renderer's
`frame` event, and only while chord's state is not `none`
(`spike-runtime-lifecycle.txt` proves the mapping with a stubbed copy).

| Transition | Done by | chord observes | chord does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Press on a selectable text | OpenTUI | `onMouse` `down`, button 0, with `getSelection()?.isStart && isDragging` | `dragging`; records `wasFollowing`, sets `moved = false`; a following view is held where it is, so new output stops moving it |
| Motion | OpenTUI | — | Nothing |
| Wheel step or scroll key while down | chord | its own scroll | Scrolls, `moved = true`, then `renderer.requestSelectionUpdate()` once `layout.ts` has moved the view, so the focus re-reads its cell at once |
| Release with text | OpenTUI | the renderer's `selection` event | `shown`; the mount window is held (below); the view follows again if `wasFollowing && !moved`; with `copyOnSelect` on, the copy starts |
| Release with no text, a click | OpenTUI | the `selection` event with `""` | `none`; no copy, no hold; the same following rule |
| A press elsewhere, a textarea edit or cursor move | OpenTUI | the next `frame`: `!renderer.hasSelection` | `none`, the hold ends; seen one frame late, which is accepted |
| The selected text gone: its renderable unmounted or emptied (a plan step dropped) | the agent's update | the next `frame`: nothing left selected | `renderer.clearSelection()`, `none`, the hold ends; the hint goes and ctrl+c is ctrl+c again (owner, 2026-10-15) |
| Any key but the copy key, a prefix or a scroll key; a paste | chord | the key's dispatch (below) | `renderer.clearSelection()`, then the key or the paste as always |
| Tab switch, `ctrl+x w`'s pick, `/clear`, `/resume`'s pick, a child picked, ctrl+o, a resize | chord | its own action | `renderer.clearSelection()` and `none` in the same step |
| The note's 3 s run out after an auto-copy | chord | `timers.ts` | `renderer.clearSelection()`, `none` (owner, 6b) |
| Quit, signal, crash | chord | the renderer's `destroy` | The copy is disposed (below) |

With `copyOnSelect` off, a shown selection stays until one of the rows
above clears it or the copy key copies it.

**Content changing under a selection** has no event, and chord neither
re-anchors nor clears on it. Clearing would fire on every streamed chunk
under a selection. What is copied is what is highlighted at the moment of
the copy: at release for auto-copy, and at the key press for the copy key.
A plan entry removed under a highlight once passed it to the entry React
drew in its place. A selection rule cannot fix that, so the plan's rows are
keyed by their content. Once the selected text is gone altogether, nothing
is highlighted, and a `shown` state would leave `ctrl + c to copy` on the
status line and ctrl+c copying nothing. So the frame watcher clears it: on
the next frame with nothing left selected, chord goes to `none`, as for a
press elsewhere (owner, 2026-10-15).

**The mount window.** CHD-327 D holds the mounted window while scrolled
back. A shown selection holds it the same way, so its entries are not
unmounted while it lasts (`mount.ts`'s held input). Held, each entry that
arrives costs about six renderables and 50 KB of heap: 3,645 renderables
and 58 MB after 600 entries, against 257 and 34 MB on the tail
(`spike-runtime-growth.txt`). With `copyOnSelect` on, the 3 s lifetime
bounds it. With it off, there is no limit (owner, 2026-10-15): a shown
selection holds the mounted entries until it is cleared, as scrolling back
does.

## Keys

| Key | With a selection shown | Otherwise |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ctrl+c | Copies; nothing else (no clear, no stop, no quit armed) | As today |
| `selection:copy` (ctrl+shift+c by default, rebindable) | Copies | Nothing |
| A chord's first key (`ctrl+x`) | Kept; waits for the next | As today |
| Esc | Clears, and interrupts or closes on the same press | As today |
| Any other key, a chord's miss included | Clears first, then does what it does | As today |
| A scroll key | Kept; scrolls | As today |
| A paste, an empty paste included | Clears first; the paste goes in at the cursor | As today |

The dispatch order with a selection, the first that answers winning:

1. `selection:copy` completed by the press (the new `Selection` context,
   live only while a selection is shown, heard before every other,
   Global's sessions included);
2. ctrl+c with no chord pending, in front of every context: with a
   permission box, a form, the search line, a picker or the agents list
   open, ctrl+c copies. The highlight is what says so;
3. a live chord's prefix: `key_pending`, and the selection is kept, so a
   rebound `ctrl+x y` copies;
4. everything else, as `boundCommand` answers it today, with a
   `clear_selection` step played first, except the scroll steps.

ctrl+shift+c arrives as ctrl+c without the kitty protocol, so it copies in
every terminal. cmd+c never reaches chord. ctrl+c stays in `RESERVED`, and
copying is the widget key's behaviour, not a binding.

**A copy, in order.** (1) Capture `getSelectedText()` synchronously in the
press's or the release's handler; empty text ends it there. (2) The state
becomes `copying` and the highlight stays. A second copy while one is out
is ignored. (3) On success, the note is shown. The copy key's selection is
cleared then; an auto-copy's selection stays for the note's 3 s. (4) On
failure, the selection is kept whatever started the copy, and the copy
key tries again.

## The clipboard

**One owner: `main.tsx`.** After `openTerminal` returns the renderer, it
builds
`createClipboard({ host: createHostClipboard({ timeoutMs: COPY_TIMEOUT_MS }),
terminal: createRendererClipboardAdapter(renderer) })`, with
`COPY_TIMEOUT_MS = 1000` as chord's own named constant. It hands `App` one
prop, `copy(text, signal) => Promise<CopyResult>`, which writes with
`destination: 'best-available'`. `selection.ts` maps OpenTUI's result, or
a rejection, to chord's `CopyResult`. The timeout is the host's
configuration; a write takes none of its own.

**One copy at a time.** A new copy aborts the one in flight and bumps a
generation. A result is noted only when it is the latest generation's and
arrives within `COPY_TIMEOUT_MS + 200` ms. A host or a fake that ignores
the abort and never settles reads as timed out at that bound. Anything
later is dropped, so no stale note appears.

**Disposal** happens on the renderer's `destroy` event, which every way
out reaches. chord bumps the generation, aborts, and calls
`clipboard.dispose()` raced against 100 ms. **Quit never waits on a
copy**: a host that ignores the abort leaves `dispose()` pending for ever
(*ran*, stub).

| Result (`spike-runtime-copy.txt`, local adapter, stub host) | Status line |
| --- | --- |
| host `written` | `copied N chars to clipboard` |
| host `failed` or `unsupported`, OSC 52 `attempted` | `sent N chars to the terminal's clipboard` |
| remote (SSH), OSC 52 `attempted` | `sent N chars to the terminal's clipboard` |
| host `timed-out` (no OSC 52 after a timeout, as OpenTUI does), or chord's bound | `could not copy: the clipboard did not answer · ctrl + c to try again` |
| over the host's 8 MiB `maxWriteBytes` | `could not copy: too large to copy · ctrl + c to try again` |
| OSC 52 `not-attempted` or `local-failure`, host not written | `could not copy: <the host's error> · ctrl + c to try again` |
| superseded, or after `destroy` | nothing |

chord applies no OSC 52 cap of its own; the service's 8 MiB bound stands.
After quit, a Linux clipboard owned by the exiting process may be lost
unless a clipboard manager took it. That is **unverified**: no Linux run
was possible, and a `written` result does not prove the text survives the
exit.

## The setting

`copyOnSelect: z.boolean()` beside `mouse` in `src/settings.ts`, in
`USER_ONLY`. One in a project's file is reported and left out, as `theme`,
`editorMode` and `mouse` are. It is read at start, with no command and no
`/config` row, because chord's `/config` lists the agent's options. Under
`"mouse": false` it is moot: no reports reach chord, and the terminal
selects as before. The guide's settings table gets a row:

```
| `copyOnSelect`                 | `false` keeps a drag's selection until ctrl+c copies it; user's file only | `true` |
```

The guide's mouse paragraph changes to:

> chord reads the mouse, so the wheel and the trackpad scroll the
> transcript, and a drag selects text. Release copies it, through the
> system clipboard, or OSC 52 over SSH. `"copyOnSelect": false` keeps the
> selection until ctrl+c copies it. To use your terminal's own selection
> instead, hold its modifier while you drag: option in iTerm2, fn in
> Terminal.app, shift in most others. Inside tmux, the wheel and drags
> reach chord only with `set -g mouse on`, and over SSH a copy reaches
> your clipboard only with `set -g allow-passthrough on`. `"mouse": false`,
> read from the user's file only, leaves the mouse to the terminal.

## States

- **Empty session.** A drag over the header's lines selects them. Over the
  mark or empty rows it starts nothing.
- **Waiting on the person** (permission, form, question, link). The
  transcript above selects as usual, and the box itself is not
  selectable. ctrl+c with a selection copies instead of stopping the turn
  and its form; once the selection is gone, ctrl+c stops it again.
- **`?` open.** The panel is not selectable. At 80×24 it covers the
  transcript, so only the draft is left to drag over.
- **Several tabs, a child picked.** A selection is the window's. A tab
  switch or a child picked clears it.
- **`"mouse": false`.** No modes, no selection of chord's, the terminal's
  own as before. The copy key finds nothing.
- **ctrl+g's editor.** The mouse modes are off while it runs, as before. A
  selection is cleared when the terminal is handed over.
- **Light terminal, `NO_COLOR`, the 16-colour theme.** The `selection`
  role's slate, with the light one (152) checked by hand.
- **Inside tmux.** Wheel and drags need `set -g mouse on`. A local copy
  goes to the system clipboard with no tmux setting. Over SSH, OSC 52
  needs `set -g allow-passthrough on`; without it the note says `sent`,
  and nothing arrives.
- **Double and triple click** select a word and a line, and copy as a drag
  does.

## Where it lives

- `src/ui/app/selection.ts` (pure, in place of `drag.ts`): the transitions
  above, the following rule, `CopyResult` and its note, the grapheme count
  (`Intl.Segmenter`), the generation and the bound.
- `src/ui/app/terminal.ts`: the one observing hook (`onMouse`'s press,
  `useSelectionHandler`, the `frame` watcher, `destroy`) and the copy.
- `src/ui/app/state.ts`, the window's half: `selection` and `copied`, in
  place of `selectHint`. `src/ui/app/keys.ts` and `keyAnswer`: the
  dispatch order, `copy_selection` and `clear_selection`.
- `src/ui/app/layout.ts`: `requestSelectionUpdate()` after its own move
  while dragging. `src/ui/transcript/mount.ts`: the held window's input.
  `src/ui/transcript/scroll.ts`: following held during a drag.
- `src/ui/app/renderer.tsx`: `enableMouseMovement: false`. `src/main.tsx`:
  the clipboard service and the `copy` prop; `selectHint`'s environment
  read goes.
- `src/ui/theme/theme.ts`: the `selection` role in every palette.
  `src/ui/theme/text.ts`: `TEXT` selectable with the role's colour, and a
  non-selectable constant for furniture. `src/ui/prompt/TextArea.tsx`:
  selectable, with shift+←/→ selection off.
- `src/ui/status/status.ts`: the note, the failure and the hint.
  `src/ui/app/timers.ts`: the note's 3 s, which clears an auto-copy's
  selection; the `drag:hint` timer goes.
- `src/ui/shortcuts/bindings.ts`: the `Selection` context and
  `selection:copy`. `keys.ts`: the line.
- `src/settings.ts`: `copyOnSelect`.
- `src/platform/`: nothing.

## Tests

- **Never the real clipboard.** Window tests get only the `copy` prop. A
  guard test fails on `createHostClipboard` or `pbcopy` anywhere under
  `tests/`: `testRender`'s renderer is not remote, so `best-available`
  from it would reach the host. The one test of the real wiring builds
  `createClipboard` with a stub host and a recording adapter.
- **Pure.** `selection_test.ts`: each transition, following with and
  without `moved`, an empty release, each result's note, generation and
  bound. `mount_test.ts`: the held window. `keys_test.tsx`: ctrl+c with a
  selection as a widget key in every context, `selection:copy` as an
  action, and a rebound `ctrl+x y`. `settings_test.ts`: `copyOnSelect`
  in the user's and the project's files.
- **Window, on the fake agent**, with raw SGR reports through the
  harness's `write` (one report a row for a drag across entries) and
  `selected()` for the text:
  - the lifecycle;
  - following stops at the press and comes back at the release;
  - a wheel step during a drag;
  - a held selection under 150 more entries;
  - superseded, late and pending-at-quit copies;
  - each failure's note;
  - `copyOnSelect` off;
  - a key and a paste over a draft selection;
  - ctrl+c copying with a permission open;
  - the 3 s lifetime;
  - `"mouse": false`;
  - the modes on a `Logged` terminal.
- **Fake agent.** `rewrap` (a reply whose last word wraps as it grows)
  and `boxlater` (a permission after a pause) are added where the ship
  team pins those outcomes.
