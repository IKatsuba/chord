# CHD-431: an asking task in the status line's hint

The owner (Q25, 2026-10-17): a `spawn_task` task waiting on the person
joins the status line's `● tester needs your permission · ctrl+x a` hint,
and `ctrl+x a` reaches its box. This supersedes the line in
[CHD-424](CHD-424.md)'s "Settled by the lead" that kept the hint as it was.

## Today

From the code and fake runs (`evidence/CHD-431/today/`, a member shown).
A task's request counts in its owner's wait, so it is reachable already. The
hint names the task's owner, or nothing, and never the task:

- **The owner is in front:** the box is on screen, there is no hint, and
  `background  ● 1 task asking`.
- **Another agent is in front:** `● main needs your permission · ctrl+x a to
  go`, and `ctrl+x a` goes to main, where the task's box is.
- **Several ask:** `● N agents wait` or `● N sessions wait`, and `ctrl+x a`
  walks them, oldest first.

## The hint names the task

When the request a session waits on is its task's, the hint names the task
as its box does (CHD-389's T1), not the owner:

| Width | Hint |
| --- | --- |
| wide | `● Agent(Fix the tokenizer) · fake needs your permission · ctrl+x a to go` |
| short | `● main's task needs your permission · ctrl+x a` |

- **For a form or a question:** `asks for input` in place of
  `needs your permission`, in both forms.
- **The short form** is used where the wide one does not fit beside the
  right side shortened, short of dropping the model. That is the existing
  order: wide words with `to go`, then short words with a bare key. At 120
  columns the wide form fits; at 80 the short one does (`mock/one-*`).
- **Why the short form names the owner:** a task's name can run to hundreds
  of characters, and the status line cuts nothing. `main's task` is short
  and true, and `ctrl+x a` goes to main's view, where the box is.
- **A member's or child's task** reads the same way, with its owner's name:
  `● parser's task needs your permission`.
- **When the owner is in front**, there is still no hint. The box is on
  screen, as for any request of the front session's own.

## Several asking

- **Nothing changes in the order.** Members, children, tabs and tasks are
  one queue, oldest wait first, and `ctrl+x a` goes to the one waiting
  longest, then the next. A task's wait starts when it asked.
- **Two or more:** `● 2 agents wait · ctrl+x a to go` (`● 2 agents wait ·
  ctrl+x a` short), or `sessions` once a tab behind is among them, as
  today. A task counts as one agent.
- **Why no new words:** the count already says how many, and `ctrl+x a`
  names each in turn as it reaches its box.

## The background summary line

It keeps `● 1 task asking` as CHD-424 draws it. The summary line counts
items, and the hint says who and how to get there. Both stay while the task
asks.
