# CHD-241: the startup splash

From launch until the first session connects, chord shows Chorded's mark,
monochrome and centred, floating gently, with one line under it. When the
session connects, the splash is cut and the window appears exactly as today,
with the CHD-203 header and its coloured mark at the top.

The frames are in `evidence/CHD-241/`, as text (`.txt`) and with the dim
escape (`.ans`, `cat` one in a terminal), at 120×40, 80×24, 44×12 and 40×7.
`sh docs/design/evidence/CHD-241/play.sh 80x24` (or `120x40`, `44x12`) plays
the drift cycle at its real timing (`<size>-float-0` to `-7` are its eight
steps), then the cut to the window. The mark
grids in them are the real generator's (`coverage` and `markGrid` from
`scripts/mark.ts` at 4 and 8 rows); the frames themselves were laid out by a
throwaway script that is not committed. `*-after-connect` and `80x24-failed`
are captures of today's chord, which is what the splash cuts to.

## The decisions, in short

| # | Question | Answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Motion | Drifts round **a small ellipse, counter-clockwise**: 4 half rows (±1 cell) up and down, 2 columns (±1) sideways. One cycle is **8 steps of 300 ms = 2.4 s**, one redraw a step. |
| 2 | Size | **8 rows × 9 columns** where the terminal has 16 or more rows and 24 or more columns (so at 120×40 and at 80×24); the header's **4-row** `MARK` from 9 rows and 16 columns; below that, **no mark**, the line alone. |
| 3 | Tone | **Dim** (SGR 2) on the terminal's own foreground, as the header's dim text. No theme token, no new one. |
| 4 | Under it | One blank row, then **`Starting <agent>…`**, dim, centred. A second line is reserved, empty until the start is slow. |
| 5 | Leaving | **A cut**, on the same render that sets the connection to ready. No transition, no minimum time. The splash **holds the screen** until then; keys pressed on it are kept for the window. Esc is the one early way out. |
| 6 | Slow, failed | Slow (5 s): the line counts seconds and the second line reads `esc for the window · ctrl+c to quit`. ctrl+c's two presses work as today, their notices taking the line. Failed: a cut to today's window with today's `did not start` line. |
| 7 | Non-TTY, `-p` | **No splash.** `-p` never mounts Ink; a non-TTY stdout gets no splash and no timer. |

## 1. Motion

The owner asked for a drift that traces a small circle, not only a bob. The
mark goes round eight places, counter-clockwise from the top centre. Down is in half
rows on the CHD-202 grid, sideways in whole columns:

```
 step        0   1   2   3   4   5   6   7      (300 ms each, then again)
 columns     0  -1  -1  -1   0  +1  +1  +1      (right of the centre)
 half rows   0   1   2   3   4   3   2   1      (down from the top)

            0
       1         7           0 top, 2 left, 4 bottom, 6 right
     2             6
       3         5
            4
```

The eight steps of the 8-row mark side by side (each in its 11 × 10 box):

```
 0            1            2            3            4            5            6            7
  ██▄         ▄▄                                                                              ▄▄
  ████▄       ███▄         ██▄          ▄▄                          ▄▄           ██▄          ███▄
  ██████▄     █████▄       ████▄        ███▄          ██▄           ███▄         ████▄        █████▄
   ▀██████▄   ▀██████▄     ██████▄      █████▄        ████▄         █████▄       ██████▄      ▀██████▄
   ▄██████▀     ███████     ▀██████▄    ▀██████▄      ██████▄       ▀██████▄      ▀██████▄      ███████
  ██████▀     ▄██████▀      ▄██████▀      ███████      ▀██████▄       ███████     ▄██████▀    ▄██████▀
  ████▀       █████▀       ██████▀      ▄██████▀       ▄██████▀     ▄██████▀     ██████▀      █████▀
  ██▀         ███▀         ████▀        █████▀        ██████▀       █████▀       ████▀        ███▀
              ▀▀           ██▀          ███▀          ████▀         ███▀         ██▀          ▀▀
                                        ▀▀            ██▀           ▀▀
```

- **The path is the circle sampled every 45°**: the column is
  `−round(sin θ)` and the half row `2 − round(2 cos θ)`. Every step moves
  the mark up or down by one half row, and four of the eight also move it a
  column. So nothing ever stands still and nothing ever jumps two places.
- **Why ±2 half rows and ±1 column.** A column is about as wide as a half
  row is tall, so this is an upright ellipse, twice as tall as it is wide:
  the shape the owner named. A ring of ±1 half row would be a true circle on
  the glass, but at eight steps it is a square of single nudges and reads
  as jitter. Going wider than one column means jumping two columns at a
  time, and that reads as a jump.
- **Half rows keep it smooth.** An odd half row pairs the grid's half rows
  differently, so steps 1, 3, 5 and 7 are different drawings (`▄▄` on top,
  `▀▀` under), not the same rows moved. Each frame is the CHD-202 grid with
  empty half rows put above it and empty columns to its left, drawn by
  `markRows(grid, { level: 'none' })`.
- **No rests.** The old bob rested at its ends because a line has ends. A
  circle has none: an even pace round it is what reads as floating, and a
  rest would read as a stall.
- **The box is the mark plus 2 rows and plus 2 columns** (11 × 10 for the
  8-row mark), centred on the screen. The mark moves inside it; the lines
  under it never move.
- **2.4 s a cycle, 300 ms a step.** It is the same slow breath as before, and
  300 ms is slow enough to stay in the corner of the eye. Every step changes
  the picture, so Ink renders 8 times in 2.4 s (about 3 a second), each a
  screen of mostly empty rows. Faster steps would need more places than the
  grid has; slower ones show the steps.
- Library first: the motion is a table of eight places and one
  `setInterval`. An animation library (spring, tween) would compute in-between
  positions the grid cannot draw. The engineer should still say what was
  tried.

## 2. Size

| Terminal | Mark | Box with the lines |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 120×40, 80×24, anything ≥ 24×16 | 8 rows × 9 columns | 10 + 1 + 2 = 13 rows |
| ≥ 16×9 (e.g. 44×12) | 4 rows × 4 columns (`MARK`) | 6 + 1 + 2 = 9 rows |
| smaller (e.g. 40×7) | none | the 2 lines |

- **8 rows needs a second grid from `deno task mark`**: `scripts/mark.ts`
  generates 4 and 8 rows, and `mark.ts` exports both (the 8-row one named,
  say, `SPLASH_MARK`). Its coverage keeps CHD-201's safety: every half cell
  is below 0.29 or at 0.59 and above, so the 0.5 threshold cannot move with a
  renderer's antialiasing.
- **Not 6 or 10 rows.** At 6 rows, sixteen half cells sit between 0.40 and
  0.67 coverage, so the shape would hang on the renderer; it is also a blob.
  At 10 rows, cells sit at 0.29 to 0.32 and a notch opens on the left edge.
- **The same 8 rows at 120×40 and 80×24**, as the header keeps one size
  everywhere (CHD-203). It is 13 of 24 rows at 80×24, and centred, with 5
  empty rows above and 6 below.
- **The 4-row mark takes the same path.** Its 4 half rows of travel are half
  its height, which is a lot, but a screen that small has little else to
  look at; the 44×12 frames show it.
- The size is chosen from the terminal's rows and columns on every render, so
  a resize recentres it and may change its size; the tick carries on.

## 3. Tone

The whole mark is **dim on the terminal's own foreground**: `<Text dimColor>`
around the rows `markRows` gives at `level: 'none'`, which are only `█`, `▀`,
`▄` and spaces with no colours. Dim is the colour of every quiet thing in
chord (the header's version and directory, the hints), it is grey on a dark
terminal and grey on a light one, and themes never paint it, so it needs no
token and every theme, `-ansi` ones and NO_COLOR included, gets the same.
Full foreground was too loud for a waiting screen; the theme's `mark` tone
would no longer be monochrome. The coloured mark then arrives with the
header, which makes "connected" the moment colour appears.

## 4. What sits under it

```
                                   ██▄
                                   ████▄
                                   ██████▄
                                    ▀██████▄
                                    ▄██████▀
                                   ██████▀
                                   ████▀
                                   ██▀
                                                  ← the two rows of travel
                                                  ←
                                                  ← one blank row
                               Starting claude…   ← dim, centred
                                                  ← reserved
```

`Starting <agent>…` is the status line's own wording today, with the
agent's name as the status line has it. Nothing else: no version, no model
(not known yet), no directory; the header says all of that a moment later.
The mark is centred on its own width, the lines on theirs.

## 5. How it leaves

- **A cut.** The render that sets the connection to `ready` draws the window
  instead of the splash. Nothing waits on the timer, and there is no minimum
  time on screen: a start that takes 200 ms shows the splash for 200 ms.
- No fade or slide: a transition would either delay the window, which the
  task forbids, or draw over a window that is already live. The splash's grey
  mark in the middle giving way to the coloured one in the corner is
  transition enough.
- **The splash holds the screen until the agent is ready** (the owner's
  answer). Keys pressed on it are kept, in order, and take effect once the
  window opens, as if typed there: letters land in the prompt, `?` opens the
  shortcuts. Nothing on the splash echoes them.
- **Esc is the one way out early.** It cuts to the window, which shows
  `Starting <agent>…` in the status line as it does today, with the kept
  keys applied; the Esc itself is not passed on. The splash does not come
  back. Esc does not quit (today, Esc does nothing during a start).
- Only the first session's start shows it. A tab opened later, `/new`, or a
  subagent starts as today.

## 6. Slow and failed starts

**Slow**, from 5 s on (`80x24-slow.txt`):

```
                                   ██▄
                                    ...
                                   ██▀


                              Starting claude… 12s
                      esc for the window · ctrl+c to quit
```

- The mark keeps drifting, so the screen never looks frozen.
- Esc is named only from 5 s on: before that, the start is quick and the
  hint would flash and vanish. Esc works from the first moment all the same.
- The first line adds the seconds, counted on the same timer; the second
  line names the two ways out. The reserved line means nothing above moves.
- **ctrl+c keeps today's two presses.** The first replaces the first line
  with `Press ctrl+c again to quit` (`*-ctrl-c-armed`), the second with
  `Stopping <agent>…` (`*-stopping`), and chord exits as today, restoring
  the terminal. Whether the second line shows or not, these are the status
  line's notices, word for word.

**Failed** (`80x24-failed.txt`): the splash is cut and the window shows what
it shows today, the header and `⏺ <agent> did not start: <message>` under
it, with the prompt and its keys. The error is never drawn on the splash:
its words can be long, and the window already lays them out, and quitting
from there is today's.

## 7. Non-TTY and `-p`

- `-p` runs headless (`src/headless.ts`) and never mounts Ink: no splash.
- When stdout is not a TTY, there is no splash and no timer: nothing should
  redraw into a pipe.

## Keys

| Key | On the splash |
| --- | --- |
| ctrl+c | first press arms, second quits (today's) |
| esc | leaves the splash for the window, early; does not quit |
| any other key | kept, and applied in the window once it opens |

The shortcuts panel lists nothing new: the splash's keys are today's, and the
slow line names them on screen.

## The owner's answers

- Tone: dim on the terminal's own foreground, as drawn.
- Motion: a drift round a small circle, not only a bob; section 1 is the
  path this design picked for it. The owner approved it (ba81da8) with one
  change: it goes round counter-clockwise, so the eight steps run in the
  reverse order (from the top centre to the left first). The frames and
  `play.sh` are in that order now.
- Keys: the splash holds the screen until the agent is ready, and keys
  pressed meanwhile are kept for the window. ctrl+c's two presses stay.
- Slow start: as designed, from 5 s, with the running count and
  `esc for the window · ctrl+c to quit`. Esc is the one way into the window
  before the agent is ready, and it does not quit.
