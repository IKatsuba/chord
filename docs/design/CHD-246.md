# CHD-246: the permission box caps its request and scrolls it

The owner (2026-10-15) calls this a safety issue. The permission box
(`src/ui/dialogs/Permission.tsx`) draws the request whole: its title,
its locations, and `inputLines` (`permission.ts`), one `key: value` per
line, every line of a value, wrapped. Nothing caps its height. An MCP
call that writes a file carries the file's content in `rawInput`, and the
box grows past the window. Then the options and the prompt box go off
the bottom of a 24-row screen, and the person cannot see what they would
allow or reach the answers. This design caps the request's part of the
box, keeps the options on screen at every size, and lets the person
scroll the request with the keys that already scroll.

Evidence:

- `evidence/CHD-246/today/mcpask-80x24.txt`: today's box on the fake
  agent's `mcpask`, a short MCP request. It fits.
- `evidence/CHD-246/mock/`: a write of a 40-line file at 80×24 and
  120×40. `before-*` is today's layout; `top-*`, `scrolled-80x24` and
  `end-*` are this design.
- **No fake scenario has a big `rawInput`.** `permission` sends
  `content: 'notes\n'` and `mcpask` three short arguments. Developer adds
  one (say `bigask`): an MCP call titled by its name, as claude-agent-acp
  sends one, whose `rawInput` holds a path, a content of about 40 lines
  with one line of about 300 characters, and a short third argument, as
  in the mocks.
- **Claude Code: no capture.** The CHD-393 captures ran in auto mode, and
  its classifier allowed every call, so no permission prompt was drawn.
  We have nothing on how Claude Code draws a long permission argument,
  and no live run was made for this.

│                                                                              │  │ the request:
│   This release changes how chord draws long requests: the permission box now │  │ capped, scrolls

│ Do you want to proceed?                                                      │  ┐

│   2. Yes, and don't ask again for files - write_file commands                │  │ never cut

╰──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────╯

(`mock/top-80x24.txt`.) The box's parts:

- **Pinned, above the request:** the asker row, when a subagent or a
  child asks, and the title's first row. A title longer than a row is cut
  at the edge with `…`. The rest of the title is the first thing in the
  request, so it is still read whole. The pinned row says what is asked
  while the person scrolls.
- **The request:** the title's rows after the first, the locations, then
  `inputLines`. This is the only part that is capped and scrolls.
- **Never cut, below the request:** the blank row, `Do you want to
  proceed?`, every option and `Esc to reject`. These are what the person
  answers with.

## The cap

The request gets the rows left once everything else under the
transcript is drawn: the working line, the plan and the queue at their
own limits, the box's border, pinned rows and answer rows, the prompt box
and the status line. It also gets **at most half the window's rows**, so
a tall window keeps some transcript above. The transcript shrinks first,
down to no rows at all, before the request loses a row.

| Window | Request rows (3 options, no asker) | Transcript rows |
|---|---|---|
| 80×24 | 6 | 0 |
| 120×40 | 20 | 2 |

A request that fits is drawn as today, with no rule and no scroll.
In a window under 24 rows the request shrinks with it, down to one row, the `↓` rule
itself, so the options still fit. The options win at every size.

## Long lines are wrapped

Every line of the request is wrapped, never cut. A cut line hides the
part of a command or a file the person is allowing, which is this task's
whole problem. Lines wrap between words, and a long word breaks at the
edge, as `wrapLine` does (CHD-393). Wrapped rows hang at the line's
indent, two cells in. The cap counts wrapped rows, so a 300-character
line costs its four rows at 80 columns.

## What shows that more is hidden

A dim rule row inside the request's room, drawn like the transcript's
`── ↓ 298 lines below · end to jump to the bottom ──`:

- `── ↓ 37 lines below · pagedown to scroll ──` as the request's last row
  while more is below.
- `── ↑ 12 lines above · pageup to scroll ──` as its first row once
  scrolled.

The key in each rule is whatever `scroll:pageDown` and `scroll:pageUp`
are bound to, spelled as every hint spells it (`sequenceHint`), so a
rebind shows the person's own key. The counts are wrapped rows. The rules take rows out of the request's
room, they do not add to it. At the end, only the `↑` rule is left
(`mock/end-80x24.txt`), and the last argument (`mode: overwrite`) is in
view. That is how the person knows they have read it all. No scrollbar:
the rules say how much and which key, and the transcript has none either.

## Keys

While the box is up **and its request overflows**, the keys that scroll
the transcript scroll the request instead. They are the same actions in
`src/ui/shortcuts/bindings.ts`, so a rebind moves both:

| Key | Action | In the request |
|---|---|---|
| pgup / pgdn | `scroll:pageUp` / `scroll:pageDown` | a page: the request's room less one row |
| shift+↑ / shift+↓ | `scroll:lineUp` / `scroll:lineDown` | a row |
| home / end | `scroll:top` / `scroll:bottom` | the request's top / its end |
| the wheel over the box | (unbound) | three rows, as the transcript's |

- ↑ ↓, j k, 1–9 and Enter keep choosing the option, as today. Esc still
  rejects.
- The wheel over the transcript still scrolls the transcript, so the
  person can look back while the box is up. When the request fits,
  every scroll key goes to the transcript as today.
- **The shortcuts panel** lists these under the lines it already has:
  `pgup pgdn or wheel to scroll`, `shift + ↑ ↓ to scroll a line` and
  `home end for the top and the bottom`. Their entries in
  `src/ui/shortcuts/keys.ts` add the box's scroll commands, and
  `keys_test.tsx` covers them. The words stay the same: they say "scroll",
  and the request is what scrolls. The panel has no spare row at 80×24
  (CHD-386), so a new line would push another out.
- The view is OpenTUI's `<scrollbox>`, per the one rule, as CHD-67 and
  CHD-95 propose for their lists. `docs/decisions.md` ("The scrollbox,
  tried") notes that its wheel is its own and that a click focuses it. In
  this box the scrollbox moving under the wheel by itself is what the
  person wants. A click taking the prompt's keys is not, and developer
  checks that. If the scrollbox does not fit, the question goes back to
  the lead, with no scroller of our own.

## States

- **Fits.** As today (`today/mcpask-80x24.txt`).
- **Overflowing, at the top.** `mock/top-80x24.txt`, `mock/top-120x40.txt`.
- **Scrolled.** Both rules (`mock/scrolled-80x24.txt`).
- **At the end.** The `↑` rule only (`mock/end-80x24.txt`,
  `mock/end-120x40.txt`).
- **A second request** waits behind the first, as today. The next box
  opens at its top.
- **Resize.** The room is computed again. The scroll position keeps its
  top row where it can and is clamped to the new end.
- **Narrow.** Every row is wrapped to the box's inner width (76 at 80
  columns). The rules are drawn to that width too.

## Colour roles

Nothing new from `src/ui/theme/theme.ts`. The border and the title use
`permission`, as today. The request is drawn in default text, and the
rules are dim. The highlight and `❯` stay the choice list's.

## For the owner: may the person allow before reading it all?

The design above works either way. This choice is the owner's, not the
designer's.

1. **Allow works at any time, as today.** The `↓ N lines below` rule says
   more is hidden, and it is the person's call.
2. **Allow waits until the request has been read to its end once.** Until
   then the allow options are drawn dim and 1–9 or Enter on them do
   nothing. The `↓` rule also says `read to the end to allow`. Reject and
   Esc always work. A request that fits is read already.
3. **The first allow while unread scrolls instead.** 1, 2 or Enter on an
   allow option scroll the request to its end and say so in the rule, and
   a second press allows.

With 2 or 3 the shortcuts panel says nothing new: the keys are the same
ones, and the rule row explains why allow waits.
