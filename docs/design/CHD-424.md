# CHD-424: monitors and tasks in a background view of their own

The owner (2026-10-17): the monitors an agent runs and the tasks it spawns
(`spawn_task`) leave the list of agents and team members under the prompt.
Under that list there is now one summary line. Choosing that line opens a
full-screen view that lists every item. CHD-2 later adds an agent's own
background tasks (Claude Code's Bash with `run_in_background`, a background
Agent) to the same view.

This is the design to build. The owner's answers on the board (Q17–Q22) set
its frame:

| Question | The owner's answer |
| --- | --- |
| Q17, where | One summary line under the agents list, wrapped and never cut. The standing screen has no rows of items at any size. The person selects the line with the keyboard and opens it, and the items show in a full-screen view at any terminal size. |
| Q18, a row | One line per item: name, kind, state with time, owner. `enter` goes to the call that started it. |
| Q19, stop | `x` stops the focused item at once, with no confirmation. |
| Q20, what moves | The agents list keeps `main`, the long-lived children, and team leads and members. Every monitor, `spawn_task` task and CHD-2 item moves, a child's too. |
| Q21, ended | An ended item stays until the person next sends a prompt in that tab. |
| Q22 | Moot after Q17. |

Everything else on this page settles details inside those answers. Each
detail gives its reason under **Why**. Where the answers leave a question of
policy open, it is listed at the end and not decided here.

**The scope is the tab.** The summary line and the view hold the items of
the front tab's agents: `main`, each long-lived child and each team member,
as today's list holds their monitors. Another tab's items are in that tab's
own summary line. **Why:** the agents list above it is per tab, and `enter`
on a row picks the item's owner. That can only be done within the tab, so
every call `enter` reaches is in this tab.

**Only items started since the session came up count.** A resumed session's
past tasks are not items, as its past monitors are not. **A closed owner's
items leave with it:** when a child or member is closed, its items leave the
summary line and the view, and are not kept as stopped.

## The standing screen

### The agents list

As today, without the monitor rows: `main`, each long-lived child and each
team's lead and members. It is drawn only while a child is open and keeps
its row limit (four at 24 rows, six at 40) and its folding. `x` on an agent
row closes the agent, as today.

### The summary line

It is the last line of the area under the status line, after the agents
list (or alone when no child is open). It is drawn while the tab has at least
one item, running or ended. Its row limit is its own; it does not count
toward the agents list's.

```
    background  ● 1 task asking · ✗ 1 monitor failed · 2 monitors and 1 task running
```

- **The words** are `background`, two spaces, then one group per state, most
  urgent first, joined by ` · `:
  1. `● N <kind> asking`: a task waiting on a permission or an input
     (warning).
  2. `✗ N <kind> failed`: a task that failed, or a monitor that ended with
     an exit code other than 0, was killed, timed out or flooded (error).
  3. `N <kind> running`: monitors running and tasks working (dim).
  4. `✓ N <kind> done`: a task that reported, or a monitor that exited 0
     (success).
  5. `■ N <kind> stopped`: one stopped by the person or by its agent (dim).

  A group with no items is left out. Within a group the kinds are listed in
  the table's order, joined by `and`: `2 monitors and 1 task running`. Each
  kind word is singular or plural by its count (`monitor`/`monitors`,
  `task`/`tasks`). CHD-2 adds its own two kind words.
- **It wraps and is never cut.** A line too long for the width breaks
  between groups, and the next line starts under the first group, past
  `background  `. A group is broken inside only when it alone is longer than
  the width. In that case it breaks between words.
- **Colour:** `background` is dim. Each group takes its state's colour, and
  the `●`, `✗` and `✓` are drawn in it too.
- **When only ended items are left**, the line keeps its ended groups
  (`✗ 1 monitor failed · ✓ 1 task done · ■ 1 monitor stopped`).
- **When it goes away:** when the person sends a prompt in the tab to any of
  its agents, every ended item of the tab is cleared, as Q21 says. If none is
  running, the line goes with them. A running item is never cleared. A
  message between agents is not the person's prompt and clears nothing.

**Why one state per group, and no names:** the line has to say at a glance
that something is asking or has failed. A name would make the line grow with
every item, and it would wrap into the rows the owner kept off the screen.

### Reaching it and opening the view

- From an empty prompt, `↓` goes into the list as today: first onto the
  agents (when a child is open), then on to the summary line, which is the
  last stop. `↑` from the summary line goes back to the last agent row, or,
  with no agents drawn, out to the box.
- On the summary line the `❯` is drawn before it (the permission colour) and
  `background` is drawn bold. The picked agent stays picked, as it does today
  when the `❯` is on a monitor.
- **`enter` on the summary line opens the view.** `x` there does nothing:
  the line is not an item.
- While the list has the keys, the status line says
  `↑ ↓ to move · enter to open · esc to go back` on the summary line, as the
  list's own hint does on agent rows. At 80 columns it shortens to `↑ ↓ move · enter open · esc back`, as the agents hint does.

**Why `enter` and no separate key:** Q17 says the line is selected the way
rows under the prompt are reached today and is then opened, and `enter` is
the key the pickers already use to open what is highlighted. A key that jumps
straight to the view would be a new global binding. It is not part of the
owner's answer, so it is listed as open below.

## The view

It takes the whole terminal: no header, no transcript, no prompt and no
status line. Esc gives them back unchanged.

```
 Background · 5 in this tab

    CI run 4812            monitor                      ✗ exit code 1 after 3m 12s · 3 events   main
    dev server errors      monitor                      running 4m 2s · 1 event                 main
    parser tests           monitor                      running 2m 3s · 4 events                parser
  ❯ Fix the tokenizer      task · fake · Fake Small     ● permission 13s                        main
    Fix the printer        task · fake · Fake Large     working 32s                             coder

 ↑ ↓ to move · enter to go to its call · x to stop · esc to close
```

- **The title**, on the first line: `Background` bold, then dim
  ` · N in this tab`, counting the items listed.
- **The rows** start on the third line, one per item, with Q18's four
  columns separated by two spaces:
  - **name:** at most 24 cells. A longer name wraps inside its column onto
    a second line (and more as needed), and the other columns stay on the
    row's first line.
  - **kind:** `monitor`. A task's kind carries its runtime and model,
    `task · fake · fake-small`, the model as the task names it, as its
    `Agent(…)` call in the transcript does.
  - **state with time**, in the state's colour (below).
  - **owner:** the agent's name, `main` dim and a child or member in its
    agent colour.
- **The `❯`** is on the focused row, in the permission colour, and that
  row's name is bold. The view opens on the first row.
- **The hint line** is the last line, dim:
  `↑ ↓ to move · enter to go to its call · x to stop · esc to close`.
- **When the rows do not fit**, the view scrolls so that the `❯` stays in
  sight. A dim `… N above` and `… N below` stand for the rows out of sight
  (`mock/view-scrolled-80x24.txt`).
- **The narrow width:** the columns give way as the agents list's do. A
  task's model goes first, then its runtime (`task · fake`, then `task`).
  The state and the owner are never dropped. When even that does not fit,
  an item takes two lines: the name and the kind, then the state and the
  owner. Every line after an item's first starts two cells further in than
  the name, so each name is the only text at the left edge. Only when the
  name and the kind do not fit together does the kind move to the second
  line. Nothing is cut.

### Sort order

Rows are in the order the items started, oldest first. An item keeps its
place when its state changes, ends or is stopped. The `❯` follows its item,
not its position.

**Why:** `x` stops at once (Q19). If rows moved by state, an item could slide
under the `❯` just before `x` and the wrong one would stop. Urgency is
already ordered on the summary line, and the colours show it on the rows.

### States on a row

| Item | State | Text | Colour |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| monitor | running | `running 4m 2s · 1 event` | dim |
| monitor | ended | `✓ exit code 0 after 3m`, `✗ exit code 1 after 3m 12s`, `✗ killed by SIGKILL after 9m`, `✗ timed out after 10m`, `✗ flooded after 2s`, each with ` · N events` | success / error |
| monitor | stopped | `■ stopped after 4m 9s · 1 event` | dim |
| task | working | `working 32s` (since it was spawned) | dim |
| task | asking | `● permission 13s`, `● input 13s` (since it asked) | warning |
| task | ended | `✓ reported after 4m 2s`, `✗ failed after 1m 12s` | success / error |
| task | stopped | `■ stopped after 30s` | dim |

CHD-2's kinds take the same shapes: `running 41s`, `✓ done in 2m 4s`,
`✗ exit code 1 after …`, `■ stopped after …`
(`mock/view-chd2-80x24.txt`).

**How a task's asking shows:** today nothing under the prompt says a task
is asking. With this design the summary line opens with `● 1 task asking`
in the warning colour, and in the view the task's row says
`● permission 13s`. `enter` on that row goes to the task's call, in its
owner's transcript, where the permission box already is.

**The times count** a second at a time, on the list's own clock
(`useAgentsClock`), while the view or the summary line is drawn. An ended
item's time stands still at its end.

### Keys in the view

- **`↑ ↓` or `j k`** move the `❯`. It stops at the first and last row and
  does not wrap.
- **`enter`** closes the view, picks the item's owner, as moving onto its
  agent row does, and scrolls the owner's transcript so that the call that
  started the item is the top line in view: `Monitor(dev server errors)` or
  `Agent(Fix the tokenizer)`. Where the transcript is too short for that,
  the call goes as high as scrolling allows, with no blank space past the
  end, as with `pgup` (`evidence/CHD-424/unit4/child-jump-120x40.txt`). The
  keys go to the prompt box, and the
  transcript stays scrolled back until the person scrolls or presses `end`,
  as after `pgup`. If the call is no longer in the transcript, nothing
  happens and the row stays focused. An item started inside a Claude
  subagent's work scrolls to its own call when that call is drawn (`ctrl+o`
  on); when the subagent's block is folded, the block's headline goes on
  top, and `ctrl+o` is left as it is. A task in a group of tasks reaches its
  own `├─` row.
- **`x`** stops the focused item at once (Q19). Its row turns to
  `■ stopped after …` in place, and the `❯` stays on it. The stop is told in
  the transcript as today (`← dev server errors · monitor · stopped`). `x` on
  an ended row does nothing. For a CHD-2 item whose adapter offers no stop,
  the hint line says `this agent offers no stop` until the `❯` moves.
- **`esc`** closes the view and returns to the standing screen with the
  `❯` on the summary line, so a second `enter` opens the view again and a
  second `esc` returns to the box.

### How a monitor's command is still reached

`enter` on its row goes to its `Monitor(…)` call, which draws `$ command`
whole and wrapped, with its id and timeout, as it does today:

```
 ⏺ Monitor(dev server errors)
   ⎿  $ sleep 1; echo "ERROR [router] POST /login 500: Cannot read properties of undefined (reading 'trim')"; echo "
      at validate (src/routes/login.tsx:41:22)"; sleep 600
      m1 · times out after 10m
```

A task's job is reached the same way, under its `Agent(…)` call.

## Colour roles

All of these come from `src/ui/theme/theme.ts`. There is no new role:

- warning for asking
- error for a failure
- success for done
- dim for running, stopped, the `background` word, the title's count, the
  hint line and `main`
- the permission role for the `❯`
- an agent colour (`agents`) for a child or member owner

## The shortcuts panel

The panel must still fit 80×24. It has no free row today (20 lines a
column), so the new `enter` line is paid for by merging the two `esc` lines
for pickers and the panel itself. No left-column
entry may be longer than 37 cells: at 38, beside the right column's 38-cell
line, the panel falls to one column at 80×24.

| Today | Becomes | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `↑ ↓ for prompts, agents and monitors` | `↑ ↓ for prompts, agents, background` | the list's own keys, not rebindable, as today |
| `x to close an agent or stop a monitor` | `x to close an agent, stop background` | `agents:close`, rebindable as today; the view's `x` is the same action, so a rebinding moves both |
| (none) | `enter to open background or a call` | the list's and the view's own key, not rebindable, as `enter` in a picker is not |
| `esc to close a picker` and `esc to close shortcuts` | `esc to close a picker or shortcuts`, the view counting as a picker | `select:cancel` and `help:dismiss`, as today |

`↑ ↓ or j k to move between choices` already covers moving in the view.
`tests/ui/shortcuts/keys_test.tsx` gets the view's keys as widget-owned keys.

## Settled by the lead

- **No direct key to the view.** It is reached only by selecting the summary
  line and pressing `enter`, as Q17 says.
- **The status line's permission hint stays as it is today.** A
  `spawn_task` task that asks shows on the summary line as `● 1 task
  asking` and on its row in the view, not in that hint.
