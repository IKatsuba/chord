# CHD-481: what a monitor is, in the background view

The owner (2026-10-20) opened the background view on four monitors `main`
had started one after another while the owner was away, all with the same
description, command and timeout, and could not tell what they were. A row
says the description the agent chose, the kind, the state and the owner;
nothing says what the monitor runs, what it said last, which one it is or
when it started.

Done (the task): the person can tell from the background view what a monitor
is and does without leaving it. What the row, or a detail for the focused
row, shows and where is the owner's choice; this doc draws three options.

Everything [CHD-462](CHD-462.md) and [CHD-424](CHD-424.md) settle holds in
each option: the pickers' frame above the prompt, capped at half the screen
(6 lines of rows at 24 rows, 14 at 40), the rows' four columns, nothing cut
(CHD-473), the rows giving way to the hint, CHD-430's scrolling by line, and
the keys `↑ ↓` / `j k`, `enter` to the call, `x` to stop, `esc`.

## Option C: the id on the row, the detail on a key (recommended)

opens its detail **in the frame, in place of the rows**; `←` or `esc` goes
back to the list, the `❯` where it was. The hint on a monitor row says so:

- **The lines**, in order: the description whole, bold as a focused name;
  the state line, the row's state in its colour, then dim the events, the
  start, the timeout and the owner in its colour; the command after `$ `,
  wrapped two in; the last batch after `← ` with how long ago it came,
  every line of it, wrapped two in, or `← no events yet`; for an ended
  monitor, chord's sentence for its end, the one the agent was sent, dim.
  Nothing is cut.
- **The title** names the item and where it is among them:
  `Background · m22, 1 of 4 in this tab`.
- **In the detail:** `↑ ↓` / `j k` go to the previous or next item's detail
  (a task's or agent's row there shows its list row alone, until those get
  a detail of their own), so four same-named monitors are compared by
  stepping; `enter` goes to its call and `x` stops it, as on the row;
  `←` and `esc` go back to the list. A detail taller than the frame's room
  scrolls with `pageup` / `pagedown` and CHD-246's rule line, as an error in
  the frame does.
- **The frame keeps its height** going in and out of the detail where the
  detail fits in it; a taller detail grows the frame up to the cap.
- **For it:** the list stays a list, a third of A's height, and at 80×24 the
  detail is whole in the six lines the cap leaves. Everything the task
  names is there: command, last event, id, start, timeout, why it stopped.
  It is how Claude Code's own background tasks dialog works: a list, a key
  into one task's command, state and output, `esc` back.
- **Against it:** one key more (below), and the command is one key away
  rather than on screen.

### The key, and an alternative without one

`→` (and `l`, as `j k` stand beside `↑ ↓`) is free in the frame: it holds
`↑ ↓ j k enter x esc pageup pagedown` today. It needs a line in the
shortcuts panel, `→ for a monitor's details`, and an action in
`src/ui/shortcuts/bindings.ts` if it is to be rebindable. **The panel is
full at 80×24** (40 entries in two columns, 23 of the 24 lines, captured by
hand on 2026-10-21): a 41st entry adds a row and needs a check that the
panel still fits, or an existing line to carry it.

The alternative, **C′: `enter` on a monitor row opens its detail**, and
`enter` in the detail goes to its call. No new key and no new panel line;
the cost is that reaching a monitor's call becomes two presses, and `enter`
means two things by kind of row. The panel's `enter to open background or a
call` would then read `enter to open background, details or a call`.

## States, in C

- **Empty:** a monitor with no events: `← no events yet`; a list with no
  monitors has no `→` in its hint.
- **Streaming:** the detail is live, as the rows are: the running time
  counts, a new batch replaces the `←` lines and the event count steps. The
  frame does not jump: a batch taller than before grows the detail inside the
  cap, then scrolls.
- **Ended while open:** the state line turns to its end in its colour and
  chord's sentence appears under the event; the `❯` stays on the item.
- **Waiting on the person:** a permission or form in the front tab hides the
  frame, detail and all, as it hides the list (CHD-462), and it comes back
  as it was.
- **Error:** a stop that fails says `could not stop: …` in the hint's place,
  whole, as CHD-462 settles; moving to another item clears it.
- **Narrow:** at 80×24 the owner's detail is six lines, the cap's six. At
  46 columns the command wraps to more lines and the detail scrolls by
  page; the state line wraps after a ` · `.

## Colour roles

No new role, from `src/ui/theme/theme.ts`: the frame, the title and the `❯`
are `permission`; the state keeps its group's role (`error` for timed out,
dim while running, `success` and `warning` as the rows have them); the
owner its agent colour; everything else in the detail and A's and B's added
lines is dim, the description bold.

## Recommendation

**C, the id on the row and the detail on `→`.** The id alone tells the
owner's four rows apart without any key; the detail then answers "what is
it and what did it do" whole, at 80×24 as well as at 120×40, without
making every row three times taller (A) or turning the list into one line
at 80×24 (B). It is the shape Claude Code's background tasks take. Whether
the key is a new `→` or `enter` (C′) is the owner's; `→` keeps `enter`
meaning one thing on every row.

## Decided

owner's words: "enter opens the item's detail in place of the rows, ↑↓ step
through items, ←/esc go back; no new key". `←` is not a new action: it is
the back `esc` already is, in the detail only.

Two things changed since the options were drawn, and the mocks below follow
them: **[CHD-479](CHD-479.md)** makes every ended monitor leave the list at
once, its end told in the transcript by its `← … · monitor · …` message, so
the owner's three timed-out rows no longer sit in the view; and **chord is
one main session per process** (CHD-493), so nothing here is drawn per tab.

- **The rows** carry the id in the kind, `monitor · m25`, as in every
  option. What tells same-named monitors apart now is two or more running

- **`enter` on a monitor row opens its detail** in the frame, in place of

- **In the detail:** `↑ ↓` / `j k` step to the previous or next item's

  the row; **`←` and `esc` go back to the list**, the `❯` on the item whose

## Open: a monitor that ends while its detail is open

Asked of the owner through the lead. What the detail does when the monitor
it shows ends (times out, exits, is stopped by its agent or by `x`):

- **(a) Stays open on the end, until the person leaves it.** The state line
  turns to the end in its colour, chord's end sentence appears under the
  last batch, `x` does nothing; on `←`, `esc` or `↑ ↓` the item is gone, and

  or the one above; the last item leaving closes the frame. The end is read
  in the transcript's `← … · monitor · …` message.
- **(c) Steps to the next item's detail** (the one above when it was the
  last; closes the frame when none is left).
