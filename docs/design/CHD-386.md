# CHD-386: word and line moves in the shortcuts panel

The owner (2026-10-14) wants option+←/→ to move the prompt's cursor by word
and cmd+←/→ to the start and end of its line, as macOS does. The task also
asks that the shortcuts panel (`?` on an empty prompt) lists them. Until
now the panel named none of the textarea's editing keys, as Claude Code
names none (the `prompt` field's comment in `src/ui/shortcuts/keys.ts`).
The task makes an exception for these two moves. Nothing else on screen
changes: the cursor moving in the box is the textarea's own drawing.

Captures of today are in `evidence/CHD-386/today/`, taken with
`bun dev chord --fake -C` on a `mktemp -d` directory, the path replaced by
`/tmp/chord-qa`. The mocks in `evidence/CHD-386/mock/` are those panels
with the two lines added and the columns laid out again as `inColumns`
and `columnCount` lay them out.

## The lines

Two new entries, in this wording:

```
alt + ← → to move by word
cmd + ← → to line start or end
```

- **Spelling.** The panel spells a modifier as `bindings.ts`'s `panelName`
  does: `alt` for option, alt and meta, `cmd` for command and super,
  lower case, ` + ` between modifier and key. Two arrows after one
  modifier are written as `shift + ↑ ↓` already is: one space, no slash.
  So not `option`, not `⌥`, and not `alt + ← / alt + →`.
- **Words.** `to …` as most lines read. `to move by word` and `to line
  start or end` are short enough that neither widens a column (below).

## Where they go

Right after `ctrl + v to paste an image` and before `↑ ↓ for prompts,
agents and monitors`. That is the run of prompt-box keys at the top of the
first column: send, the two newlines, the editor, paste, then the cursor's
moves. `↑ ↓` straight after them reads as the next move in the same box.

## It fits

- **80×24.** 42 entries in two columns are 21 rows; with the prompt box's
  three that is 24, the whole screen. Today the panel is 20 rows and one
  row is left over (`today/panel-80x24.txt`); the two lines take it.
  Width: the columns need 2 + 37 + 3 + 38 = 80, the same as today, since
  the widest lines (`x to close an agent or stop a monitor`, `ctrl + c to
  stop the turn and its form`) stay in their columns. Both new lines are
  shorter than either. This leaves **no spare row at 80×24**: a later line
  has to share a line with another, as `esc to close commands or files`
  does.
- **120×40.** Three columns of 14, 118 columns wide.

## What does not join them

- **ctrl+a and ctrl+e** stay off the panel. They are readline's keys,
  which the panel leaves out as Claude Code does, and `ctrl + a` is already
  on it as the resume picker's `to show all directories`; a second
  `ctrl + a` line would read as a clash. And 80×24 has no row for them.
- **alt+b and alt+f** get no words of their own. They are the same word
  move, as some terminals send option+←/→ (`ESC b`, `ESC f`). Like
  `ctrl + j` under `shift + enter for a newline`, they go in the entry's
  `prompt` presses, so the key test covers them, and the line on screen
  names the arrows a person presses.
- The entries' `prompt` presses are whatever the textarea is made to act
  on for the move (for example `alt + ←`, `alt + →`, `alt + b`, `alt + f`,
  `cmd + ←`, `cmd + →`). That list follows what developer finds terminals
  send; the two lines on screen stay as above.

## Colour and keys

The lines are drawn as every panel line is: the key and its words in the
panel's existing roles, nothing new from `theme.ts`. Neither line is
rebindable by the keybindings file unless the unit adds actions for them;
if it does, the line names the bound keys as the other entries do, and
these words stay.

## States

The panel has one state, open on an empty prompt, and the two lines
look the same in every state of the session. A terminal that sends
nothing for cmd+←/→ still shows the line: the panel says what chord
answers, as `shift + enter` is listed where a terminal cannot report it.
